Maxim Autos: Winning on the Website

Design only, 2026-07-25. Nothing here has been published, posted, deployed, or sent. Not one file on the live site was touched by this run. Everything below waits on your yes.

The honest verdict

You asked for the best dealership site inside 50 miles. You cannot have it, and buying it would be a waste.

Maxim will not outrank Carvana, CarMax, Napleton, Berman, or CarGurus on the big searches. Three measured reasons, none of them fixable by anything on your site. One: you have 22 links from 5 other websites, and cancelling CarsForSale takes that to 4. The aggregators have six figures. Two: 28 of your pages sit in Google's own report as found and not fetched. Better writing on a page Google refuses to open changes nothing. Three: 7 cars, 5 to 8 sales a month, one person. Price and radius searches are locked up by the aggregators and stay that way no matter what we build.

What is winnable, and you already win some of it today. Every search that names something only Maxim does. Measured this morning: "used car dealer Skokie CVR metal plates same day" returns Maxim in 6 of 10 results, and "used car dealer with financing for all credit levels Skokie Illinois" returns Maxim at position 1, ahead of UR Approved and Napleton Honda. The target is to own all of that ground, be the cleanest and fastest independent dealer site in the Skokie set, and be the dealer an answer engine can read without guessing.

Your real rival is iCars Chicago on Dempster, not Carvana. And the number this plan is judged on is not sales. At 5 to 8 cars a month with swings of plus or minus 3 already, a genuine 30 percent lift in leads is 1 to 2 extra cars. That is invisible in the sales number for 90 days and arguably for a year. Anyone who tells you otherwise is selling you something.

The one sentence that matters. Every dealer reachable inside 50 miles runs a rented vendor platform: Sherman Dodge on Dealer eProcess, Napleton Honda and Ultimo Motors on Dealer Inspire, iCars on DealerSocket, Best Auto Sales on Carsforsale.com, Old Orchard Nissan on WordPress. A tenant cannot ship custom data, custom machine readable content, or a custom front end even if they want to. You own your source, so you structurally can. That is the moat: not a schema type, not a page count. Owning the code is what lets you ship a build gate that refuses false copy, one place where every number lives, and one clean picture of the business.

What is broken right now

Illinois law, live

You advertise a price range no car on your lot fits

"$5,000 to $15,000" appears on 15 pages, including the machine data Google reads and the answer text on your own FAQ. Your cheapest car is $8,995 and your top car is $15,995. Nothing you own sits inside the range you publish.

This is not a typo, it is an Illinois advertising rule broken in public (14 Ill. Adm. Code 475.390). Worse, the guardrail document that exists to prevent this hardcodes the wrong band itself, under the heading "use verbatim everywhere".
Live in Google's feed

A protection promise you do not offer, 62 times

"Every customer protected" sits in the footer of 56 pages, closes all 7 live car descriptions, and appears 7 times inside the feed Google ingests. Your brand document does not merely contain the phrase, it orders it as the closing line of every car description.

It promises coverage on every car when the real protection is Illinois statutory powertrain on qualifying cars only. Google Merchant Center has already disapproved this account three times.
Source document

Your own playbook teaches a false warranty

copy-playbook.md cites a 3 month warranty in 15 places. On line 533 it teaches that warranty as the approved replacement for the banned word "certified".

Any agent that correctly dodges one banned word lands on a false claim by instruction. Fixing the website while this file is loaded just reintroduces the claim next week. It gets retired, not edited.
Money

Your analytics counts clicks, not customers

The lead event fires the moment a finger touches a submit button, before the form is actually sent. A fumbled tap counts exactly the same as a real lead.

You cannot tell which cars or which pages produce calls, so every decision about the site is a guess. Nothing about leads in this plan is provable until this is fixed, which is why it is near the front of the queue.
Money

Six phone numbers nobody can tap

On six of your busiest pages the number sits in a box that renders nothing at all on a narrow phone, and is not clickable on a wide screen either.

Calling is the highest intent thing a used car shopper does, and on a phone your number is not on the page. Six positions in your analytics cannot ever fire, so any nonzero phone number after the fix is brand new signal.
Money

The financing button sends nervous buyers to a Social Security form

Three buttons on every car page, including the sticky bar labeled Pre-Qualify, go straight to the 60 field application that demands an SSN. That page also states the SSN is "never stored on our servers", while the code encrypts and stores it.

Credit shy buyers bail at the SSN, and you carry a false privacy statement on your own page. Two real credit applications exist in three months, one of them your own test.
Found by Google

Two copies of your homepage compete

The old GitHub Pages mirror serves a byte identical homepage with nothing telling Google to ignore it, and it points at itself as the original.

Google gets two candidates for the same page and picks one. Your production site should never have to argue with a leftover.
Found by Google

8 pages nobody can reach, 2 dead buttons on the homepage

8 of your search pages have zero links pointing at them from anywhere on the site. Two homepage chips point at addresses your own robots file blocks. One page targets Audi, a make you have never had in stock, live or sold.

Pages with no path in do not get crawled and do not rank. The Audi page also sits squarely inside the language the FTC used in its March 2026 letters about advertising cars you do not have.

Nine pages render car cards carrying an estimated monthly payment with no payment footnote anywhere on the page.

That is a federal lending disclosure rule, not a style preference, and it blocks any new page that shows a car card until it is fixed.

What the red team took out of this exact mockup

Removed: the big summed totalThe design put "Estimated out the door $17,471" in a navy bar right here. Illinois tax follows your buyer's registration address, so a Chicago buyer is about $200 higher and the published total is wrong for them. On the one page whose entire promise is that the number you see is the number you pay, a total that is low at signing is the single worst thing we could publish. Removed, not softened.
Removed: the fixed 7.25 percent Skokie tax figureThe rate is a property of the buyer, not of your lot. So the line stays and the number goes, replaced with the plain sentence about where they register.
Removed: the second payment columnThe design showed $305 a month next to $336 with tax and plates financed. The $336 is derived from the total, so it dies with the total. The single estimate that is already live on your car pages stays, with its disclosure directly underneath it.
Kept: the fee as a priced line, which is the whole pointYour zero moves from a claim into the middle of the arithmetic, where a shopper can feel it against the $377.63 every other store charges. Every input is already in your data. No photography, no transcription, no weekly chore, no new tool.
Kept: the honest buttonThe three buttons that currently dump a nervous buyer into a 60 field SSN form get retargeted to the soft financing page, carrying the car with them. The full application stays available for a buyer who is ready.
Gate before this shipsThe title fee, the plate fee, and the doc fee cap each get confirmed against a primary Illinois source. All three live in one small file with a source and a date per line, so that is a five minute check, not a code hunt. If the cap cannot be confirmed, it ships with no number at all: "Illinois allows dealers to charge a documentary service fee. We charge zero." That loses almost nothing.
